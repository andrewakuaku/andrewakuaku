About this project

Betrothy is for love and relationship, whatever stage you are at. If you are single, meet someone: not a stranger from a swipe, but someone from your network, or someone cool and like-minded. If you are exploring a relationship, build it together: the early days are for finding out who you are to each other, and Betrothy gives the two of you somewhere to do that.

Love is closer than it looks. You have a network strong enough to find a partner, and Betrothy puts that network to work for you.

What you can do

  • Meet someone. Your network: see who your friends know, and how close each person is to you. Blind dates: voice-only dates, paired on the night. Events: mixers, game nights and nights out, hosted by people on Betrothy. Betrothals: introduce two people you know, or let a friend introduce you.
  • Build it together. A question a day: Decision Formation asks you both one written question each day, and shows you how you each think. A page for the two of you, with your story and what you are up to, for the people who know you. Surprises for the two of you: ideas and activities picked for you and your partner, so there is always something new waiting. Nights out and trips away, from the events people host to the ones you make yourselves.
  • A song of your own. Pick a genre or describe a mood, then type or sing your idea. The studio writes the lyrics, records the vocal, and designs the cover art into a finished song.
  • For students. Busy semester? Meeting people shouldn't be. Sign up with your student email and Betrothy shows you other students. Blind dates and events that fit between classes, with no swiping.

How Betrothy works

Through the people you know. Friends, churches, schools and workplaces each hold a network of people who know one another. Betrothy draws on those networks and makes it straightforward to connect through them.

  • Friends. Your friends, and their friends in turn, already know one another and one another's families. You reach the people they know.
  • Churches and schools. A congregation or a school shares a place, a routine and years of acquaintance. You meet others inside a community you already belong to.
  • Workplaces and events. Colleagues, former colleagues and collaborators, and the events that bring that network together, open to a city or to an invited group.

Why we built Betrothy

Love is socially mediated. A Stanford study of how American couples meet tracked every path to a partner since 1940. For most of that time the leading paths ran through people a person already knew. In 1995, 33 percent of couples had met through friends, 15 percent through family and 19 percent through work. Two percent had met online.

By 2017, 39 percent of new couples had met online. Meeting through friends had fallen to 20 percent, through family to 7 and through work to 11. Yet for all the ease of meeting online, friends kept a significant hold, and stayed the second most common way new couples met. Friends still introduce one couple in five (Rosenfeld, Thomas and Hausen, How Couples Meet and Stay Together, Stanford, 2019).

People already do this. People prefer to meet through the people and places they know, even on networks that discourage it, and Betrothy takes that as its starting point. Every person a member finds is shown with the connections they share and how far apart they are in the network. A school, a congregation or a workplace in common is the link between two people.

Betrothy leverages the online experience to scale the role that friends, churches, schools and workplaces play in mediating love, bringing love to everyone.

My role

I founded Betrothy and build it end to end: the product, the network that ties singles and couples into one community, the AI tooling, and the brand, from the hand-drawn wordmark to the wine, cream and gold system. It runs on the web and as native iOS and Mac apps. My UC Berkeley capstone on AI in dating is the research behind it.
